# TrendingTesla Visual Standard v1.0

## General visual identity

Create high-quality, photorealistic editorial imagery for TrendingTesla, an independent international media platform covering Tesla, technology, energy, robotics and space exploration.

The imagery must be visually attractive, technically credible and suitable for a professional technology publication.

### Core visual characteristics

- Photorealistic editorial photography.
- Realistic proportions, materials and physical details.
- Natural but vibrant colours.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Sharp, prominent primary subject.
- Clean composition with controlled background detail.
- Attractive lighting, preferably natural daylight or golden hour when appropriate.
- Warm highlights balanced with rich blue tones.
- Subtle red, cyan or yellow accents where naturally appropriate.
- Professional photographic composition.
- Realistic reflections, shadows and perspective.
- Strong subject separation without excessive artificial blur.

Avoid grey, washed-out, excessively desaturated or visually lifeless imagery.

### Technical accuracy

When depicting identifiable vehicles, robots, spacecraft or other real products:

- Preserve the correct product generation and variant.
- Match verified reference photographs.
- Maintain correct proportions and component placement.
- Do not invent charging ports, door mechanisms, displays, cameras or other technical features.
- Do not combine design elements from different generations.
- Cars are left-hand drive on the right side of the road (US and continental Europe): steering wheel and driver on the left.
- If an important detail cannot be established reliably, choose a composition that does not expose that detail.

### Text and branding

Unless specifically requested:

- No readable text.
- No invented logos.
- No watermarks.
- No readable licence plates.
- No artificial labels or numbers.
- No incorrect interface text.

Authentic product branding is acceptable when appropriate and accurately represented.

### Composition

The primary subject should remain clearly recognisable.

Allow sufficient space for responsive cropping.

Do not place important details near the image edges.

Use realistic camera angles and photographic depth of field.

Avoid unnecessary objects that distract from the subject.

## Profile A — Editorial

Purpose: website articles and daily news.

- Natural, credible editorial photography.
- Moderate contrast and realistic lighting.
- Accurate depiction of identifiable products.
- Clear subject and relevant environment.
- No exaggerated cinematic effects that suggest an event occurred differently from reality.

## Profile B — Cinematic

Purpose: shorts, video scenes and thumbnails.

- Strong visual impact and clear focal point.
- Attractive golden-hour or dramatic lighting when appropriate.
- Rich, natural colours and controlled contrast.
- Dynamic but physically credible composition.
- Background detail must support the story without distracting from the subject.

## Profile C — Technical

Purpose: engineering, batteries, software, factories and component explanations.

- Technical accuracy takes priority over visual spectacle.
- Use verified references for identifiable components.
- Preserve correct geometry, connections and relative dimensions.
- Use lighting that reveals construction details clearly.
- Avoid fictional components, misleading cutaways or unsupported engineering features.

## Journalistic integrity

AI-generated images must not be presented as authentic photographs of specific news events.

For accidents, fires, legal proceedings or other sensitive events, use authentic licensed imagery where available.

Otherwise, generate a clearly illustrative scene that cannot reasonably be mistaken for documentary evidence.

For unannounced products and prototypes, depict only confirmed features.

Record image provenance and generation metadata.

## Output requirements

- Landscape editorial images: normally 1536×1024.
- Vertical shorts: normally 1024×1536.
- Use the appropriate colour profile and image compression for publication.
- Maintain enough resolution for the intended use.
- Avoid unnecessary generation when an existing approved image is suitable.

## Quality control

An image must pass all three assessments:

1. Visual quality.
2. Technical correctness.
3. Journalistic suitability.

Visual quality must score at least 7/10.

Critical technical or journalistic errors always cause rejection, regardless of the overall score.

Do not publish an image merely because it looks attractive.
