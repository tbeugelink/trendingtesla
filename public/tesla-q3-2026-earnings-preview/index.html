Tesla Q3 2026 earnings tonight: the numbers and questions that matter

Tesla reports third-quarter results after the market closes today. Here is what Wall Street expects and which topics will decide how the stock reacts.

TrendingTesla · October 21, 2026 · 2 min read

Tesla publishes its third-quarter 2026 results today, Wednesday October 21, after the US market closes. The live call with Elon Musk and the management team starts at 5:30 p.m. Eastern Time (4:30 p.m. Central, 11:30 p.m. in Central Europe). The update deck appears on Tesla's investor relations site shortly before.

What we already know: deliveries

Tesla shared its delivery numbers at the start of October. The company handed over 486,532 vehicles in the quarter. That is about 2% fewer than a year earlier, but clearly above the roughly 462,000 analysts had expected. Almost all of it came from the Model 3 and Model Y; all other models together accounted for only about 8,300 cars, roughly half of last year's level.

The energy business grew more slowly than hoped. Tesla deployed 13.7 GWh of storage, almost 10% more than a year ago, but below the roughly 15.9 GWh the market had penciled in.

What Wall Street expects

  • Revenue: around $28 billion, essentially flat compared with the same quarter last year.
  • Earnings per share (adjusted): about $0.45, roughly 10% lower than a year ago.
  • The spread is wide: individual analyst estimates for revenue range from about $25 billion to more than $30 billion, a sign of real uncertainty.

Expectations are cautious for a reason. Last quarter Tesla earned $0.33 per share, well short of the $0.53 analysts were looking for, while spending heavily: management has guided to more than $25 billion in capital expenditure for 2026.

The questions that will move the stock

For Tesla, the call usually matters more than the numbers. The most popular shareholder questions this time are about the future rather than the past:

  • Cybercab: how fast the dedicated robotaxi fleet will scale and when ordinary customers can use it.
  • Unsupervised FSD: a concrete timeline for self-driving without a safety driver.
  • Robotaxi economics: what a robotaxi ride actually costs and earns.
  • Optimus: the expected cost per humanoid robot.
  • HW3 owners: what happens to cars with older self-driving hardware.

Expect questions about the new Roadster as well. Tesla unveiled the long-delayed sports car last week and said it was developed together with SpaceX, which has fueled fresh speculation about closer ties between Musk's companies.

Why it matters

Tesla's car business is not growing at the moment, so the story investors are buying is autonomy, robotics and energy. If management can show credible progress and dates on Cybercab and unsupervised FSD, weak profit figures may be forgiven. If the answers stay vague while costs keep rising, the pressure on margins will be hard to ignore.
