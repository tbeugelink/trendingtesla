Thirty engines doomed the Soviet moon rocket. The N1 flew four times and failed four times. They never fired together on the ground, and one failure could cascade. SpaceX puts thirty-three Raptor engines under Super Heavy. Four reasons explain the difference. A giant engine is slow to develop: Apollo's F-1 fought combustion instability for more than three years. It cannot throttle down far enough to land. Thirty-three smaller engines can switch most of themselves off, and Super Heavy ends on its three centre engines. On one giant engine, a failure ends the flight. A cluster can carry on when the failure stays contained. And the whole set is lit on the pad first, the test the N1 never ran.
